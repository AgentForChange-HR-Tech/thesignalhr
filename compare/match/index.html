    The landscape

    Four places a score comes from

    Scores are now in most recruiting stacks, often switched on in a release rather than bought. Ownership and litigation shape the risk: Workday owns HiredScore, SAP owns SmartRecruiters and is moving its recruiting there, Employ owns Lever, and Eightfold faces a class action over how candidate profiles are built. Assessments, video interviews and chatbot screening are out of scope here; they carry their own rules.

    Score inside an ATS

    The match or ranking feature in your applicant tracking system. Often the cheapest to switch on, and the one most often switched on without a governance review.

    Greenhouse Talent Matching · Ashby criteria evaluation · Lever Talent Fit · iCIMS candidate ranking · SmartRecruiters Winston Match

    Score inside an HCM suite

    Scoring inside Workday, Oracle or SAP SuccessFactors. One data model with the HCM; the AI is frequently a separate SKU or consumption-priced.

    Workday HiredScore · Oracle Recruiting recommendations · SAP SuccessFactors applicant screening

    Talent intelligence platform

    Skills-inference platforms that score applicants, past candidates and employees against roles, usually on top of your ATS.

    Eightfold match score · Phenom Fit Score · Beamery Talent Match · Avature matching

    Sourcing and screening tool

    Tools that rank sourced prospects and, increasingly, inbound applicants, next to your ATS. Watch where the profile data comes from.

    Gem AI Application Review · hireEZ Applicant Match · SeekOut AI Recommendations · Findem applicant matching

    Greenhouse Talent MatchingScore inside an ATSPrivate (Greenhouse Software)

    Best for: Teams on Greenhouse that want a governed, explainable first pass over inbound applicants. Not for: Teams that want a score to run on top of a different ATS.

    Under 500 · 500–5,000 · 5,000+ employees · Scores inbound applicants, past candidates · Requires Greenhouse Recruiting

    What the recruiter sees

    Sorts applications into tiers of fit and shows which job criteria each résumé matches.

    How the score is set

    Recruiters choose which job criteria to prioritize and how much weight each gets.

    Automatic actions

    Cannot automatically advance or reject candidates.

    Governance

    • Explains each scoreDocumented
    • Recruiter-set criteriaDocumented
    • No auto-rejectDocumented
    • Can be switched offNot documented
    • Published bias auditRecent, public
    • AI certificationCovers the product

    Audit and certification evidence

    ISO/IEC 42001 (February 2026). Warden AI continuously tests Talent Matching for disparate impact across 10 protected classes, gates each release on a passing audit and publishes results.

    Strengths

    • Recruiter-weighted criteria with per-candidate matches shown
    • Warden AI tests every new version before release and publishes results on a public dashboard
    • ISO/IEC 42001 certified (February 2026)

    Watch-outs

    • Whether and where it can be switched off isn't documented publicly
    • Past-candidate matching arrived in June 2026; check which plan includes it
    • Tiers are not a decision: make sure recruiters still read below the top tier

    How it's priced

    Part of Greenhouse's recruiting plans; confirm which tier includes Talent Matching.

    Ask in the demo

    • Show the criteria and weights behind one candidate's tier, and how a recruiter changes them.
    • Can we turn Talent Matching off for one country or job, and is that change logged?

    Verified October 2026 · Sources: Greenhouse bias audit statement · Greenhouse release notes, June 2026 · Greenhouse ISO/IEC 42001

    Ashby criteria evaluationScore inside an ATSPrivate (Ashby)

    Best for: Teams that want AI to check criteria without collapsing candidates into a single number. Not for: Buyers who need a published, current bias audit or an AI certification.

    Under 500 · 500–5,000 employees · Scores inbound applicants, past candidates · Requires Ashby

    What the recruiter sees

    Meets, does not meet or uncertain for each recruiter-defined criterion. No overall score or ranking.

    How the score is set

    Recruiters define criteria per role; a language model checks each résumé against each criterion.

    Automatic actions

    Outputs are reviewed criterion by criterion; automatic actions aren't documented.

    Governance

    • Explains each scoreDocumented
    • Recruiter-set criteriaDocumented
    • No auto-rejectPartial
    • Can be switched offPartial
    • Published bias auditOlder or narrower
    • AI certificationNone found

    Audit and certification evidence

    FairNow audited the criteria-evaluation model under NYC Local Law 144 (August 2024): no group below the four-fifths threshold. No AI certification found.

    Strengths

    • No single score to anchor on: recruiters see criterion-level results
    • 'Uncertain' when the résumé doesn't say, instead of a guess
    • Independent NYC bias audit published (August 2024)

    Watch-outs

    • The only published audit is from August 2024 and covers one model
    • AI Talent Rediscovery (May 2026) uses metered credits
    • AI tools are opt-in; customers carry notice and consent duties

    How it's priced

    AI features in Ashby's plans with credit allowances by tier; rediscovery uses credits per candidate returned.

    Ask in the demo

    • When will you publish a 2025 or 2026 audit, and will it cover rediscovery?
    • Can a 'does not meet' result trigger any automatic action in our workflows?

    Verified October 2026 · Sources: Ashby bias audit by FairNow, August 2024 · Ashby terms for AI features · Ashby: AI Talent Rediscovery

    Best for: SmartRecruiters customers, on SAP or not, that want an explained score across a large applicant flow. Not for: Teams that need to set the criteria and weights themselves.

    A match score labeled Very high, High, Medium or Low, with a plain-language explanation.

    How the score is set

    Automatic actions

    Automatic rejection isn't documented.

    Governance

    • Explains each scoreDocumented
    • Recruiter-set criteriaNot documented
    • No auto-rejectPartial
    • Can be switched offNot documented
    • Published bias auditNone found
    • AI certificationCovers the product

    Audit and certification evidence

    Announced ISO/IEC 42001 in August 2025 (certifier not named) and says its AI passes an annual NYC Local Law 144 audit; no report found.

    Strengths

    • Explanations of why a candidate does or doesn't match
    • Multi-dimension scoring with a 35,000+ skill taxonomy, per SmartRecruiters

    Free to use

    The 12-question match-score RFP bank

    Questions that show whether you could explain a score to a candidate, a regulator or a works council. If the vendor can't answer them, you won't be able to either.

    1. Show, for one of our jobs, exactly what a recruiter sees next to a candidate's score, and what would change it.
    2. Who sets the criteria and weights: us, the model, or past hiring outcomes? Can we see and lock them per job?
    3. Can the score automatically reject, advance, hide or contact anyone? What are the defaults, and who can change them?
    4. Can we switch scoring off by country, state or job, and is every change logged?
    5. Send the latest independent bias audit: auditor, date, data, the features covered and the impact ratios.
    6. Will you give us score and outcome data for our own jobs so we can run our own audit?
    7. Which data feeds a candidate's profile beyond what they submitted, and can we switch each source off?
    8. What do you provide for our candidate notices (NYC, Illinois, Colorado, California, Ontario, EU)?
    9. Which AI certification do you hold, who issued it, and does its scope name this feature?
    10. How do you test a new model version before release, and how will we know it changed?
    11. Is scoring included in our license, a separate SKU, or consumption-priced?
    12. If a candidate asks why they were rejected, what can we tell them from your system?
    Independence

    How we review

    • No vendor pays. No sponsorships, affiliate links or paid placement, and no vendor reviews its profile before publication.
    • Scope. Scores, grades and rankings computed from applications, résumés and profiles. Assessments, games, video interviews and conversational screening are out of scope: they work differently and carry their own rules.
    • Ratings reflect public documentation. "Documented" means the vendor documents it publicly; "Partial" means claimed without detail, or limited; "Not documented" means we found nothing published, so ask. We rate the score as sold, not a test of the product.
    • Bias audits. "Recent, public" means an independent audit or continuous monitoring of the scoring feature, published within about the last 18 months. "Older or narrower" means older, undated, a single model, or hosted by a customer. Audit claims without a published summary count as none found.
    • AI certification. "Covers the product" means an independent certification of the vendor's AI management, such as ISO/IEC 42001 or TrustArc, that covers the vendor or the product. "Narrower scope" means its scope doesn't name the product.
    • Rules where you hire. 17 jurisdictions with laws or guidance that bear on hiring scores, each linked to a source. It's orientation, not legal advice.
    • ATS integrations. Listed only when we found them on a marketplace, partner or help page, not from a vendor's "integrates with every ATS" claim.
    • Pricing. We describe how each vendor charges and where costs grow, not list prices, because enterprise prices are negotiated and change.
    • Freshness. Every profile shows when it was verified. We re-verify quarterly and after major vendor or legal news. Spot something out of date? Tell us and we'll fix it with a note.
