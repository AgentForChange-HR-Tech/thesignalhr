    What will set your go-live date

    We don't publish implementation timelines, because the honest answer depends more on your organization than on the platform. A vendor's timeline usually starts at kickoff and assumes a customer team that is ready and available. Your clock starts the day you choose a vendor, and it runs through approvals, staffing and your own calendar before anyone configures anything.

    Before kickoff · usually missing from vendor timelines

    Fixes, adoption and phase-two features. Usually months, not weeks.

    Phases overlap in practice. Running security, privacy, AI and contract reviews during selection, rather than after it, is one of the most effective ways to pull your date forward.

    What vendor timelines leave out

    • The stretch between choosing and kickoff: security, privacy, AI and legal reviews, procurement, and waiting for consultants.
    • Your people's real availability: the plan assumes fast decisions and test sign-off from a team that also has a day job.
    • Your calendar: peak hiring, year-end, open enrollment and the other projects competing for the same people.
    • Stabilization: the months after go-live when fixes, adoption and phase-two features happen.

    Check your drivers

    14 questions in four groups. Answer what you know. Each answer shows whether it is likely to drive your date and what helps, and your summary and question list build below.

    Resourcing

    Who does the work, and how much of their time is really free.

    Delivery model

    Who will do the build?

    Each delivery model has its own delay. A partner adds a second selection and a statement of work before anyone starts, plus three-way coordination. An internal build moves at the speed of your team's experience with the platform. Vendor teams often run several customers at once.

    What helps

    Agree who decides, who builds and who tests before kickoff, and write it into the statement of work. The gaps between parties are where weeks disappear.

    Your team's time

    How much time can your project lead and analysts give?

    Every design decision, test script and data check waits on your people. Vendor plans assume you turn things around in days. A team doing this on top of open requisitions, other projects and production support turns them around in weeks.

    What helps

    Backfill the project lead and the HRIS or TA-ops analyst for the length of the project. Agree turnaround times for decisions and test sign-off, and track them like any other milestone.

    Consultant staffing

    Is the vendor or partner team staffed?

    Vendors and partners usually assign consultants after the contract is signed. In busy periods, such as the run-up to a suite's major release or year-end, waiting for an experienced consultant can be the first delay, before any work starts.

    What helps

    Make named consultants and a start date part of the signed order, with the right to approve substitutions.

    Reviews and approvals

    What has to be approved before anyone can configure anything.

    Security review

    What does your IT security and third-party risk review involve?

    Security review usually can't start until you've picked a vendor, and it can't finish until the vendor answers. Findings that need remediation reopen it. It is one of the most common reasons a chosen platform sits still.

    What helps

    Send your security questionnaire with the RFP. Ask finalists for their SOC 2 Type II report, penetration-test summary and subprocessor list before demos end, and book the architecture review slot before you choose.

    AI review

    Will you use AI that screens, ranks, scores or interviews candidates?

    AI that influences who moves forward needs its own approvals, on a legal timetable rather than the project's: your AI governance review, a bias audit before use in New York City (Local Law 144), California's automated-decision rules (compliance by January 1, 2027) and EU AI Act high-risk obligations for recruiting AI (now December 2, 2027). Colorado, Illinois and other states add notice duties.

    What helps

    Go live with selection-influencing AI switched off and add it as a second phase once governance signs off. Ask every finalist for an inventory of the AI features in your tier, and whether each can be turned off by country or state.

    Countries and works councils

    Where are the candidates this system will cover at go-live?

    Each country adds a privacy review (a data protection impact assessment under GDPR in Europe), data-transfer terms, consent and retention rules, and local languages and forms. Where there are works councils, their agreement on new HR systems and selection rules is needed before go-live, on their meeting calendar. In Germany that includes systems that can monitor performance and AI-supported selection guidelines.

    What helps

    Brief works councils and data protection officers during selection, not after signature. Roll out country by country, starting where approvals are simplest.

    Contracting

    How does your company contract for new software?

    Redlines on the data processing agreement, AI terms, liability caps and data return happen after you choose and before kickoff. Budget approval tied to a fiscal cycle can park a decision that's already made.

    What helps

    Send your data processing agreement and AI terms with the RFP so redlines run alongside demos. Confirm when the budget is approved before you start the evaluation.

    Scope

    How much you are connecting, moving and changing.

    Integrations

    How many systems must connect at go-live?

    Each integration needs design, build and end-to-end testing with a third party that has its own queue and release schedule. Hire sync to the HRIS matters most: if it's wrong, new hires don't get paid. Suite recruiting avoids hire sync but leans on your HRIS team instead.

    What helps

    Go live with the integrations you need on day one and schedule the rest as phase two. Confirm early that each third party has a current, supported connector, not one on the roadmap.

    Data migration

    What will you move from your current system?

    Migrating history means mapping fields, cleaning data, deciding retention and consent country by country, and running several test loads. It is also the part of the plan vendors tend to size least.

    What helps

    Move open work only, and keep history in a read-only archive under your retention rules. Decide this before design, not during testing.

    Process variation

    How standardized is your hiring process?

    Requisition approvals, offer rules and compensation sign-offs are where most projects slip, because they force decisions the business has put off. Five variations take longer to design, test and train than one.

    What helps

    Agree one standard process before design starts, and make every exception justify itself to a named decision-maker.

    Org readiness

    How fast your organization decides, learns and makes room.

    Decision speed

    Who signs off design decisions?

    Design produces dozens of decisions a week. If each one waits for a committee meeting, the meeting calendar sets the pace, not the work.

    What helps

    Give one owner the authority to decide, with a short escalation path, and keep the steering committee for scope, budget and go-live calls.

    Training and change

    How many people need to learn the new system?

    Recruiters learn a new ATS quickly. Occasional users, mostly hiring managers, need short guides, translated materials and help at the moment they use it. That work is often missing from the vendor's plan.

    What helps

    Design the hiring-manager experience first and keep it short. Budget change management and translated job aids as their own workstream.

    Competing calendar

    What else is happening during the project?

    The people you need for the project are the same people needed for campus season, a holiday ramp, year-end, open enrollment or an HCM upgrade. Suite customers also have to test around the vendor's scheduled releases.

    What helps

    Pick the go-live window first and plan backwards from it. Never cut over during peak hiring.

    Hard deadline

    Is there a hard deadline, such as your current contract ending?

    A forced cutover is the most expensive way to go live: scope gets cut late, testing gets squeezed and adoption suffers.

    What helps

    Negotiate a short extension on your current system now, while you still have leverage, and count it as part of the project budget.

    The landscape

    Five kinds of platform

    2025–26 reshaped ownership: Workday bought Paradox, SAP bought SmartRecruiters, Thoma Bravo took Dayforce private and Phenom made three acquisitions in 2026. Ownership changes roadmaps, so it's on every profile below.

    Standalone ATS

    A recruiting system that sits beside your HRIS. Best recruiter experience and fastest innovation; you own the HRIS integration.

    Greenhouse · Ashby · Lever · Gem · Workable · iCIMS · SmartRecruiters

    HCM suite recruiting

    The recruiting module of your HCM suite. One data model and no hire sync to build; recruiter experience and pace depend on the suite.

    Workday Recruiting · SAP SuccessFactors Recruiting · Oracle Recruiting Cloud · UKG Pro Recruiting · Dayforce Recruiting

    High-volume conversational

    Conversational apply, screening and scheduling for hourly roles. Usually runs alongside a core ATS.

    Paradox

    Talent intelligence & CX layer

    Talent intelligence, CRM and candidate experience above or beside the ATS. Decide first whether you're replacing or layering.

    Avature · Phenom · Eightfold

    Agency ATS

    Built for staffing firms: clients, submittals, placements and pay-and-bill.

    The 13-question RFP bank

    Questions that separate demo theater from deployed reality. Ask them in this order; the discomfort is diagnostic.

    1. Show me, live, how a recruiter moves a candidate from application to offer. Count the clicks.
    2. What does implementation require from my team, in hours per week, for how many weeks, and from which roles?
    3. Which of your AI features make or influence selection decisions, and where is your bias audit for each (NYC Local Law 144)?
    4. Which AI features can we switch off by country or state, and who in your company can change that setting?
    5. Which AI features are generally available in our tier today, versus beta, preview or roadmap?
    6. How are AI agents metered and billed, and what caps or alerts do we control?
    7. What's the all-in three-year cost for our volume, including modules and the integrations you assume we'll add in year two?
    8. Which three integrations do customers most regret assuming would 'just work'?
    9. Show hire sync into our HRIS with a real offer, including a change after acceptance.
    10. What happens to our data, in what format, by when and at what cost, if we leave?
    11. What did your last three price increases look like for existing customers at renewal?
    12. Who owns our account after go-live, and how many accounts do they manage?
    13. What's on your roadmap that is dated and committed, versus directional?
    Independence

    How we review

    • No vendor pays. No sponsorships, affiliate links or paid placement, and no vendor reviews its profile before publication.
    • Production versus promise. AI is listed as "in production" only when the vendor says it is available; everything else is labeled announced, beta, preview or roadmap with its date.
    • Capabilities. "Native" means built into the product; "partial or add-on" means a separate module, a partner or limited depth; "not a strength" means we found no strong evidence, so ask.
    • AI certification. "Certified" means an independent body certified the vendor's AI management (such as ISO/IEC 42001 or TrustArc). "Partial" means a published independent bias audit, or a certification whose scope doesn't name the product. Claims without published evidence count as none found.
    • Implementation. We don't publish timelines. They depend more on your resourcing, reviews, scope and readiness than on the platform, so we show what drives them and the questions that expose them. Delivery models describe how each platform is usually implemented; ask who will actually do yours.
    • Pricing. We describe how each vendor charges and where costs grow, not list prices, because enterprise prices are negotiated and change.
    • Freshness. Every profile shows when it was verified. We re-verify quarterly and after major vendor news. Spot something out of date? Tell us and we'll fix it with a note.
