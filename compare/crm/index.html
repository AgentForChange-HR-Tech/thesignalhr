Step 1

Do you need a separate CRM?

Most ATS platforms and HCM suites now include a CRM, and many teams already pay for one they barely use. A separate CRM pays off when there is enough pipeline work to do and someone to do it. Answer what you know; the verdict, the capabilities to prioritize and your question list build below.

What you need it for

Whether a CRM has enough work to do to pay off.

Repeat hiring

How often do you hire for the same roles?

A CRM earns its keep when you build a pipeline once and hire from it many times. One-off roles rarely justify nurture campaigns.

What to look for

Prioritize nurture campaigns and rediscovery, and ask to see one pipeline used for several hires.

Hard-to-fill roles

How hard are your hardest roles to fill?

Scarce skills reward warming people up before a role opens. Roles that fill from applicants don't need a CRM to do it.

What to look for

Prioritize AI sourcing and rediscovery, and check how much matching runs on your own database versus outside data.

Events and campus

Do you run campus recruiting, events or talent communities?

Event registration, check-in and follow-up are where ATS talent pools run out first.

What to look for

Prioritize event registration, mobile check-in and follow-up campaigns; many ATS CRMs cap or leave out events.

Internal talent

Should the CRM cover employees and alumni too?

Treating employees and alumni as a talent pool needs employee profiles and matching, which many CRMs don't have.

What to look for

Prioritize internal mobility; outside the suites and talent intelligence platforms, few CRMs do it well.

What you already own

What CRM do you already have?

Many teams already pay for a CRM module in their ATS or HCM suite and use a fraction of it. Using it well costs less than buying another.

What to look for

List what you already license before any demo. The cheapest CRM is often the module you aren't using yet.

Can you run one?

Whether your team, data and ATS can keep a CRM alive after launch.

Campaign owner

Who will write and run nurture campaigns?

A CRM without a campaign owner turns into a database. Content, segments and send schedules are ongoing work, not a setup task.

What helps

Name a campaign owner before you sign and budget content time each month. Start with one segment and one campaign.

Recruiter habits

How do your recruiters source today?

A CRM changes daily recruiter habits. Teams that don't source today rarely start because a new tool appears.

What helps

Pilot with a team that already sources, set a weekly pipeline target, and expand from their results.

Data quality

How clean is your candidate database?

Rediscovery and matching only work on records that are current, deduplicated and consented. Bad data makes AI suggestions look wrong.

What helps

Import only recent, consented, deduplicated records and archive the rest. Agree a duplicate rule with your ATS before go-live.

ATS connection

How will the CRM connect to your ATS?

Prospects, applications and status changes must move both ways without creating duplicates. A broken sync means recruiters work in two systems.

What helps

Ask for a reference customer on your exact ATS and watch the sync live, including what happens when a prospect applies.

What has to be approved

The reviews a CRM triggers that an ATS often doesn't.

Consent and contact rules

Who will you contact, and how?

Keeping and contacting people who never applied needs a lawful basis. US texting needs consent and carrier registration; GDPR needs a lawful basis and retention limits for talent pools; California's privacy law covers job applicants too.

What helps

Build consent capture into every form and event, set retention by country, and check text-message carrier registration before launch.

AI review

Will AI match, rank or contact candidates?

AI that ranks or contacts people can count as an automated employment decision tool: New York City requires a bias audit before use, California's automated-decision rules apply from January 1, 2027, and EU AI Act high-risk obligations follow on December 2, 2027.

What helps

Ask for the bias audit covering the exact features you'd use, and keep AI-drafted outreach under recruiter approval at launch.

Security and privacy review

What does your security and privacy review involve?

A CRM holds personal data on people who never applied, often enriched from outside sources. Security and privacy teams look harder at that than at an ATS.

What helps

Send your questionnaire with the RFP, and ask where enriched contact data comes from and whether each source can be switched off.

Questions to ask, by who answers them (19)

Questions behind the gaps you flag are marked Priority and move to the top.

The vendor
  • Show one pipeline being used to fill the same role twice, with the second hire's source tracked. Repeat hiring
  • Run a search for one of our hardest roles live: where do the profiles come from, and how many are already in our database? Hard-to-fill roles
  • Show an event from invitation to check-in to follow-up campaign, and which parts cost extra. Events and campus
  • Show an employee and an external prospect matched to the same role. What does the employee see? Internal talent
  • Which campaign templates and playbooks are included, and who builds our first campaigns? Campaign owner
  • Show a recruiter's daily view: how many clicks from a LinkedIn profile to a prospect in a sequence? Recruiter habits
  • How do you deduplicate against our ATS, and which system wins when the two disagree? Data quality
  • Show a prospect applying in our ATS and the status flowing back to the CRM, live. ATS connection
  • How are consent, opt-outs and retention expiry handled by country, and can we prove consent for one contact? Consent and contact rules
  • Which AI features rank or contact candidates, and where is the bias audit for each? AI review
  • Which data sources enrich our prospect profiles, and can we switch each one off? Security and privacy review
Employer brand or marketing
  • Who owns campaign content and results, and how many hours a week do they have for it? Campaign owner
Your IT security team
  • What does a system holding prospect data from third-party enrichment need to pass, and how long is the queue? Security and privacy review
Legal and privacy
  • Which past candidates can we legally contact again, and for how long? Data quality
  • What consent and retention rules apply to talent pools in each country we recruit in? Consent and contact rules
  • Do any of these features count as an automated decision tool where we hire? AI review
Your TA leadership and recruiters
  • Which CRM features do we already license in our ATS or suite, and which are we using? What you already own
  • Which team pilots it, and what weekly pipeline target will they be measured on? Recruiter habits
  • Who on our HRIS or ATS team owns the integration after go-live? ATS connection

    The landscape

    Five kinds of CRM

    2025–26 reshaped ownership: SAP bought SmartRecruiters and now points its recruiting future there, Workday bought Paradox, Harver bought Symphony Talent, Clinch is now sold as PageUp Clinch under EQT, and Phenom made three acquisitions in 2026.     Gem

    Talent intelligence platform

    Beamery · Phenom · Eightfold · Avature

    Recruitment marketing CRM

    Recruitment marketing CRMs: career sites, employer brand, campaigns and events, usually layered on a suite ATS.

    Radancy · Symphony Talent · PageUp Clinch

    The 13-question CRM RFP bank

    Questions that separate a demo's best campaign from what your recruiters will do on a Tuesday. The discomfort is diagnostic.

    1. Show a past applicant from a year ago being found, added to a campaign and tracked to hire. Count the clicks.
    2. Which CRM features are in our quote, and which (texting, events, career site, AI matching) are add-ons, seats or consumption?
    3. How does the CRM sync with our ATS: which records, in which direction, how often, and which system wins a conflict?
    4. How are consent, opt-outs and retention handled by country, and can we export proof of consent for one person?
    5. Which AI features rank, match or contact candidates, and where is the independent bias audit for each?
    6. Which AI features are generally available in our tier today, versus beta, preview or roadmap?
    7. Where do enriched contact details come from, and can we switch each source off?
    8. How many of your customers have more than half their recruiters active in the CRM every week?
    9. Which campaign templates, content and playbooks are included, and who builds our first campaigns?
    10. How is source of hire attributed when a prospect touched three campaigns and an event?
    11. What happens to our talent pools, campaign history and consent records if we leave: format, timing, cost?
    12. What did your last three price increases look like for existing customers at renewal?
    13. What's on your CRM roadmap that is dated and committed, versus directional?

    • Production versus promise. AI is listed as "in production" only when the vendor says it is available; everything else is labeled announced, beta, preview or roadmap with its date.
    • Capabilities. Ratings describe the CRM as sold in its CRM tier or module. "Native" means built in; "partial or add-on" means a further module, a partner or limited depth; "not a strength" means we found no strong evidence, so ask. What needs an extra license is in each profile's pricing and cost traps.
    • AI certification. "Certified" means an independent body certified the vendor's AI management (such as ISO/IEC 42001 or TrustArc). "Partial" means a published independent bias audit, or a certification whose scope doesn't name the product. Claims without published evidence count as none found.
    • ATS integrations. Listed only when we found them on a marketplace, partner or help page, not from a vendor's "integrates with every ATS" claim.
    • Implementation. We don't publish timelines. They depend on your campaign capacity, data, consent and ATS sync more than on the platform, so the Step 1 check shows those drivers and the questions that expose them.
    • Pricing. We describe how each vendor charges and where costs grow, not list prices, because enterprise prices are negotiated and change.
    • Freshness. Every profile shows when it was verified. We re-verify quarterly and after major vendor news. Spot something out of date? Tell us and we'll fix it with a note. Every material change is in the change log.
