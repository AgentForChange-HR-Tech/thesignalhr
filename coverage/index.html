Regional radar

What's changing where you hire

The developments HR leaders at multinationals are acting on now, region by region. Use the region filter above to narrow the view.

Americas

The January 1, 2027 AI-hiring cliff

Colorado's rewritten AI law and California's privacy-agency rules both start January 1, 2027: notice before AI influences a hiring decision, an explanation or appeal route, and records. Federal preemption efforts haven't displaced either. Read the analysis →

Sources: Epstein Becker Green · Bass Berry

Ontario made the job posting a compliance document

Since January 1, 2026, Ontario employers with 25 or more staff must say in public postings whether AI screens, assesses or selects applicants, post a pay range no wider than $50,000 and drop Canadian-experience requirements.

Sources: Norton Rose Fulbright

Latin America is cutting the workweek on fixed dates

Chile moved to 42 hours on April 26, 2026. Mexico steps down to 46 hours on January 1, 2027, on the way to 40 by 2030. Colombia's Sunday premium rises to 100% in July 2027. All without pay cuts.

Sources: Pauta · Expansión Política

Federal agencies stepped back from disparate impact. States didn't.

The EEOC's June 2026 enforcement plan stops disparate-impact investigations, but private Title VII claims remain, and New Jersey codified disparate-impact rules that cover automated tools in December 2025.

Sources: Epstein Becker Green · Morgan Lewis

Europe, Middle East & Africa

EU pay transparency: the deadline passed, the reporting year didn't

Only Italy, Malta, Slovakia and Lithuania transposed the directive by June 7, 2026, and the Commission refused to delay it. First pay-gap reports are due June 7, 2027, on 2026 pay. Read the analysis →

Sources: Trusaic tracker · Ius Laboris

The EU AI Act moved, but parts already apply

Employment AI's high-risk duties now start December 2, 2027. Emotion recognition at work has been banned since February 2025, and chatbot disclosure has applied since August 2, 2026. Draft Commission guidance treats candidate ranking as high-risk when scores drive the decision. Read the analysis →

Sources: White & Case · Mayer Brown

UK employment law keeps changing in stages

Day-one statutory sick pay arrived April 6, 2026. The duty to prevent sexual harassment, including by third parties, starts October 30. The unfair-dismissal qualifying period drops to six months on January 1, 2027.

Sources: GOV.UK

The Gulf tightens workforce nationalization

Saudi Arabia's Evolved Nitaqat (April 2026 to December 2028) ties required Saudi percentages to actual headcount and adds profession quotas, such as 60% for marketing and sales roles. UAE firms with 50 or more staff pay monthly fines for each unfilled Emirati role.

Sources: Al Tamimi · Gulf News

Asia-Pacific

Australia's automated-decision disclosure starts December 10

Privacy policies must disclose decisions made, or substantially assisted, by computer programs when they significantly affect people. The OAIC published guidance on September 30, 2026. Candidate screening is likely in scope. Read the analysis →

Sources: OAIC · Gilbert + Tobin

Korea classes hiring AI as high-impact

The AI Basic Act took effect January 22, 2026, with explanation, human-oversight and notice duties for high-impact AI, which includes hiring. Fines are deferred for at least a year.

Sources: Cooley · U.S. International Trade Administration

India's labour codes are law; the states are catching up

The four codes took effect November 21, 2025: a 50% wage floor that drives provident fund and gratuity costs, gratuity for fixed-term staff after one year, and final settlement within two working days. Central wage and industrial-relations rules were finalized May 7, 2026.

Sources: iPleaders · Outlook Business

Japan widens harassment and pay-gap duties

Since October 1, 2026, every Japanese employer must take measures against customer harassment and harassment of job-seekers. Employers with 101 to 300 workers must now disclose their gender pay gap, starting with fiscal years from April 2026.

Sources: Orrick: harassment · Orrick: pay gap
